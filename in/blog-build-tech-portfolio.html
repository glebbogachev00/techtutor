How to Build a Tech Portfolio Before High School

It's not just about job prep; it's about showcasing creativity, passion, and the amazing things they can build

By Stef Nguyen • January 13, 2026 • 8 min read
Home › Blog › How to Build a Tech Portfolio Before High School

So, what to include in a student portfolio? Anything they've created with technology! The goal is to show a range of skills and interests. Don't worry about projects being "perfect", the effort and learning are what count.

Here are some fantastic project ideas to get started.

What Goes Into a Tech Portfolio for Kids?

1. Foundational Coding Projects for Kids

This is the heart of many portfolios. Starting with block-based coding and moving to text-based languages builds a strong foundation.

  • Scratch Animations & Games: Scratch is a free, visual programming language from MIT. Kids can create interactive stories, games, and animations. A collection of 3-5 polished Scratch projects is a fantastic starting point.
  • A Simple Website: Using HTML and CSS, a child can build a website about their favorite hobby, a book report, or even a site dedicated to their pet! It demonstrates practical design and coding skills.
  • Beginner Python Scripts: After getting comfortable with the basics, kids can try simple but fun projects in Python. For ideas, check out our guide to fun Python projects for kids.

2. Design and Multimedia Creations

A student tech portfolio isn't just about code. Creative design skills are just as valuable and showcase a different kind of tech fluency.

  • Graphic Design with Canva: Using a free tool like Canva, kids can design logos, social media posts, presentations, or posters. This demonstrates an eye for design and digital communication.
  • Video Editing: A short, edited video, whether it's a book trailer, a gaming highlights reel, or a stop-motion movie, shows storytelling ability and technical skill with editing software.

3. Game Development & World-Building

Many kids are drawn to tech through gaming. Channel that passion into creation!

  • Roblox Game Creation: Instead of just playing Roblox, kids can use the free Roblox Studio to build their own "obbies" (obstacle courses) or full games. This is a fantastic example of early tech education in action. Learn how to get started with our Roblox Studio Beginner's Guide.
  • Minecraft Mods: For kids who love Minecraft, learning to create simple modifications ("mods") or designing intricate structures are impressive technical achievements.

How to Showcase the Portfolio

Once your child has a few projects, they need a place to display them. You don't need anything fancy or expensive.

  • A Simple Portfolio Website: Using an easy website builder for students is the best way to go. Google Sites is 100% free and user-friendly. For each project, include a short description of what it is, what they learned, and a link or video of it in action.
  • Another fantastic option is the Canva Website builder. It's perfect for creating a visually stunning, single-page portfolio with drag-and-drop ease, which is an excellent choice for design-focused kids who want their showcase site to look as creative as their projects.
  • A GitHub Profile (For Coders): As kids get more serious about coding, creating a GitHub profile is the next step. It's the industry standard for programmers to store and share code.

The Big Picture: Why Starting Early is a Game-Changer

Encouraging an early tech education through a portfolio does more than just teach technical skills. In an increasingly competitive landscape, it provides a significant edge.

The demand for tech skills is exploding. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects that computer and IT occupations will grow much faster than the average for all occupations in the coming decade. Colleges know this and are actively seeking students who can demonstrate readiness for this future.

This is why admissions officers are looking for more than just grades. While a 2023 NACAC report shows grades and curriculum strength are still top factors, the landscape for competitive schools is different. For a pool of applicants who all have high GPAs, a passion project is what makes a student memorable. Admissions officers at top universities often state that a link to a thoughtful portfolio or a well-maintained GitHub profile can be a massive differentiator, showcasing the initiative, creativity, and resilience that grades alone cannot.

Building a tech portfolio for kids isn't about pressure. It's about providing a fun, creative outlet that empowers them to build their ideas and be proud of what they can accomplish. Start small, celebrate every project, and watch their confidence and skills grow into a powerful asset for their future.
