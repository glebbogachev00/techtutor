A typical path

Scratch→GDevelop→Roblox→Website Creation→Startup Foundations

Nobody has to do all of them, and plenty of children start in the middle. The pattern that works is: blocks first, then typed code, then something with real users attached. A child who starts at twelve usually skips straight to GDevelop or Roblox.

Choosing between two

They play games constantly

GDevelop or Roblox. Roblox especially — it converts the thing they already do for hours into something they publish. It is the shortest distance between an existing habit and building.

They like drawing more than machines

3D Designer. It rewards visual judgement first and brings the technical side in gradually, and the weekly output is an image you can look at.

They want to make something people use

Website Creation, then App Development. Both end with a link or an install you can open on your own phone, which is a very different motivation from a score.

They are curious about AI

Generative AI Magic first — it is about using the tools with judgement. AI & Programming Quest is the harder one: Python, trained models and bias, and it wants some coding behind it already.

They have an idea they keep talking about

Startup Foundations. It is the only program where the goal is a real user rather than a finished exercise, and it assumes they can already build a little.

What parents usually ask next

How long does one program take?

24 lessons across 3 levels, 90 minutes each, usually 2–3 sessions a week. Each level of 8 lessons ends with the student presenting what they built.

What if we pick the wrong one?

Commitments are portable across every program, so nothing paid for is lost if your child moves. In practice the concepts overlap heavily — sequence, loops, variables and events appear in all of them.

Does my child need any experience?

Not for Scratch, GDevelop, Roblox, Website Creation or 3D Designer. AI & Programming Quest and Startup Foundations go faster if there is some coding behind them.

What do we need at home?

A laptop or desktop and Chrome. Every tool used across the nine programs is free to download, and most of what your child builds can be opened on your own phone.

How do I know it is working?

Every program produces something you can look at or open — a playable game, a live URL, a rendered image, an app on a phone. If you cannot see it, ask; that is the point of the showcase lessons.
