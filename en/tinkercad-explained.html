Program explainer

What is Tinkercad?

Tinkercad is 3D design built for beginners, and the whole thing rests on one idea small enough for a six-year-old to hold in their head: every shape is either something you add, or something you take away. Two taps below and you'll have it.

Try it — make a donut

Drag the picture to spin it around

That is genuinely it. Add a shape, subtract a shape, group them. Rockets, monsters, cookie cutters, a name keyring — everything children build in this program comes from repeating those three moves. The grown-up CAD software used by architects and engineers works on exactly the same logic.

What they'll actually make

Real objects, every lesson

A donut, a rocket, a butterfly, their own monster, a cookie cutter, a treasure chest with a lid that actually fits. Nothing is left half-finished between weeks — each session ends with something named and spinnable.

Their own name in 3D

Chunky 3D letters pulled up out of the workplane, sat on a plate, with a hole punched for a keyring. This is usually the design families ask to print first.

Models built by code

In the final level children move to Tinkercad Codeblocks, where models are assembled by dragging instruction blocks. A single Repeat block builds a whole staircase — their first real taste of why programmers bother with loops.

Why choose this program

It genuinely works at age six

No typing, no syntax, almost no reading. Everything is drag, drop, add, subtract — and lessons are taught by demonstration, so an early reader is never held back by the interface.

You can see the progress

Every design has a share link that opens on your phone. You can spin your child's work yourself without understanding a single thing about the software.

Spatial thinking, early

Turning objects in your head, breaking a complicated thing into simple parts, working out what fits inside what — the groundwork for later maths, engineering and design.

It leads somewhere

Tinkercad is made by Autodesk, so it is a real first rung rather than a toy. Children move on to Blender in 3D Designer, or sideways into Scratch and the programming tracks.

The tool: Tinkercad — free, made by Autodesk, and runs in an ordinary web browser with nothing to install.
