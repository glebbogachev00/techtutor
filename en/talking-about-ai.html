Talking to your child about AI.

Your child is already meeting AI at school, in apps and through friends. You don't need to be an expert to guide them. You need one sentence and three conversations.

What AI is, in one sentence you can reuse

"AI has read a huge amount of text and pictures, and it guesses what should come next. It's very good at guessing, but it's still guessing."

That sentence works for an 8-year-old and a 15-year-old. It explains why AI is useful, and why it is sometimes wrong while sounding completely sure.

Good at

  • Explaining an idea a different way
  • Giving a first idea to start from
  • Checking spelling and structure

Not good at

  • Being right every time
  • Knowing your child, their class or their teacher
  • Doing the thinking that makes learning stick

Three conversations to have this week

"It can be wrong."

Prove it together. Ask an AI chat something you both know well: about your family, your street, or your child's school. Watch it get details wrong, confidently.

"Let's see if we can catch it making a mistake."

Children remember a mistake they caught far better than a warning they were given.

"Think first, then ask."

For homework, agree on one simple rule: your child tries first, then uses AI to check or explain. Never to produce the answer.

"Show me your try first. Then we'll ask the AI what it thinks."

"Some things stay private."

Full name, school, address, photos of themselves, passwords: none of these go into an AI chat.

"Would you tell this to a stranger? Then don't tell it to the AI."

Worth knowing: most AI chat apps set a minimum age of 13 or older. Younger children should use them with you, not alone.

The goal isn't to keep AI away.
It's to raise a child who questions it.
