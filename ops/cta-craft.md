# The Close

The hooks and the stories have been getting good. The closes have not. This file exists
because a post that earns attention for twelve lines and then dies in the last three has
wasted all twelve.

## The diagnosis, from a real draft

This was a genuinely good post that fell apart at the end:

> Every child has a different rhythm. What we do is find that rhythm first, then build the
> lessons around it. That is the whole approach.
>
> If this sounds closer to home than you expected, you can book a free trial class. One
> session, and we will put together a study plan that fits your child's actual pattern, not a
> template.
>
> Text us on Zalo: Đăng nhập tài khoản Zalo

Five separate failures, and they are the common ones:

**1. The platitude paragraph.** "Every child has a different rhythm… That is the whole
approach." The post had just made a sharp, specific, arguable point about difficulty and
attention. This throws it away and replaces it with something no parent could disagree with,
and therefore something no parent will act on. **A generic summary immediately before the ask
is the single most reliable way to kill a close.** Delete it. The specific point IS the setup.

**2. Hedged permission instead of an instruction.** "If this sounds closer to home than you
expected, you can book…" Two hedges in one sentence: a conditional opener, and "you can",
which grants permission rather than asking for action. Nobody was waiting for permission.

**3. Two asks.** Book a trial class, and text us on Zalo. A reader given two doors walks
through neither. One ask, one channel.

**4. A pasted link title doing the work of a CTA.** "Đăng nhập tài khoản Zalo" is Zalo's login
page title, scraped in by the paste. It is not a call to action, it is debris.

**5. No picture of what happens next.** "Book a free trial class" leaves the parent to imagine
the process, and imagination invents friction — how long, who calls me, will I be sold to.

## What a close has to do

Four things, in one to three lines:

1. **Name the one action.** One verb, one channel.
2. **Tie it to the specific thing the story was about**, not to a general benefit.
3. **Say what happens next**, concretely enough that there is nothing to imagine.
4. **Remove the risk**, plainly, if it is genuinely free.

## Rules

**Command, do not permit.** "Message us on Zalo" beats "you can message us on Zalo". Drop
"feel free to", "why not", "if you're interested", "you might want to".

**No conditional opener.** "If this sounds like your kid…" weakens everything after it. The
story already established relevance; re-asking is a retreat. If you want the reader to
self-identify, do it with a concrete question, not a hedge: "Ask him what happens when a game
stops getting harder."

**One ask, one channel.** Zalo or the comments or the link. Never two.

**Make the first step small.** The purchase is 8,400,000₫. The ask is not. Ask for a message,
a reply, a question, one free class. The smallest true next step converts better than the real
one, and it is more honest about what actually happens next.

**Be specific about what they get.** "One free class, and afterwards we send you what he
actually did in it" is a picture. "Book a trial" is an errand.

**Reach back into the story.** The best closes use the story's own detail as the trigger.
Generic: "if you want your child to be more engaged." Specific: "if your son drops a game the
second it stops being hard."

**Say it costs nothing, if it does.** Free is the strongest word available and it is true here.
Do not bury it mid-sentence.

**A reason to act now, only when true.** A real cohort date or a real remaining spot can be
stated flatly. Never invent one. No deadline is better than a fake deadline.

**No link debris.** Never paste a raw URL or a page title into the copy. "Link in the comments"
or "message us on Zalo" — the platform handles the rest.

## The fixed version of the example above

> The teacher sets the work one level past where Minh sits. On purpose.
>
> Ask your son what he does when a game stops getting harder. If the answer is that he quits,
> that is the same thing.
>
> Message us on Zalo and we will put him in one free class this week. Afterwards we send you
> what he actually built in it, and where we would pitch him next.

One ask. One channel. The trigger comes straight out of the story. What happens next is
concrete. Free is stated plainly. No platitude, no hedge, no debris.

## Before you ship a close, check

- Is there a general statement about children or learning in the last four lines? Cut it.
- Does it start with "if"? Rewrite it.
- Count the asks. More than one, cut to the best.
- Could a parent picture exactly what happens after they act? If not, say it.
- Is the trigger from this story, or could it sit under any post? Make it from this story.
- Is there a raw link or a pasted page title? Remove it.
