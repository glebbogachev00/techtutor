# Image Craft — four kinds of image, four different jobs

Gleb's observation, and it is correct: an Ogilvy product photograph, a direct-response
demonstration shot, and a modern brand ad are not the same tool. They fail in different ways
when misused. This file sorts them and says which are available to us.

Derived from `~/Documents/ads`: the Rolls-Royce and Hathaway ads, the Big Brand Ads set, and
Gleb's own generated TechTutor images.

---

## Type 1 — The Receipt

**The reference:** Gleb's own two generated images. A plain chat thread, monospace, black on
white, showing a mother's actual messages: *"Why aren't you guys giving him homework?" / "Is he
even studying?" / "I saw him playing games during the class."* Then a typing indicator.

**What it does:** proof and instant recognition. It is Throssell's documentary receipt as a
picture. Any parent who has ever had that doubt sees their own message.

**Why it works, and this is the important part:** it is completely unstyled. No palette, no
illustration, no mascot. The credibility comes *from* the plainness — styling it would make it
look authored, and an authored receipt is not a receipt.

**Two rules it breaks on purpose:**
- **Text is the content.** The blanket "no text in images" rule does not apply to this type.
  The words are the image.
- **No brand palette.** Monochrome. Do not add `#193B92`, do not add Mochi orange. Leaving it
  unbranded is the technique.

**The open loop.** The typing indicator at the end is the strongest thing in either image. The
answer has not arrived. That is the visual form of "leave them unsatisfied" — perfect for a
Job C post, where the copy must not resolve either.

**Use it for:** the objection posts (`fb_myth_buster`, `fb_parent_faq`), proof, and any
promotional post whose hook is a doubt in the parent's own words.

**Never fake a receipt.** Only real messages, real code, real project screens. A fabricated
screenshot of a message nobody sent is the worst thing on this list — it is inventing evidence,
not inventing atmosphere.

## Type 2 — Story Appeal

**The reference:** the Hathaway shirt. A man in a shirt, in a shop, wearing an eyepatch. The
eyepatch has nothing whatsoever to do with shirts. It made an unknown brand famous because you
cannot look at it without inventing a story about him.

**What it does:** stops the scroll with one unexplained detail in an otherwise ordinary scene.
The viewer does the work.

**How to build it:** take the plain scene the post is about, then put in the one odd, specific,
true thing. Not a metaphor — an oddity. The cat with the white speech bubble saying no. The
project named Harry Bum Cheeks on screen. The chicken from the platformer level.

**Use it for:** `laugh`, `curious`, IG reel moments. This is the type that suits our best short
posts, and it pairs exactly with the "find the detail he thinks is boring" rule.

Brand palette and no-text both apply normally here.

---

## Type 3 — Honest Demonstration

**The reference:** the Rolls-Royce. The car photographed in a real street outside a real shop,
people walking out, no studio lighting. Then the headline is a factual quote and the body is
thirteen numbered facts. Price stated plainly.

**What it does:** creates desire for the actual thing by showing it truthfully. It does not
flatter the product; it documents it.

**Our version is the kid's actual work.** The real Scratch screen. The real game running. The
real app the sibling uses. Not an illustration *of* a kid coding — the artefact itself. And not
prettified: a real screen with the mess still in it is more persuasive than a clean mockup.

**Use it for:** `fb_student_showcase`, `proof`, `try_this_home`.

---

## Type 4 — Mascot Brand Wit — NOT AVAILABLE TO US

**The reference:** the Duolingo underground poster. The owl's face at enormous scale, flat red
and green, a three-word joke, no product, no copy, no information at all.

**Why it works for them:** near-universal brand recognition, a captive audience with two seconds
of attention, and a free app one tap away. The poster only has to trigger recall.

**Why it fails for us, and it would fail expensively:**
- We have no brand recognition to trigger. There is nothing to recall.
- The decision is high-consideration. A parent is choosing where their child spends their
  evenings and paying millions of dong. Wit alone does not move that.
- We have no captive audience. We are in a feed, competing on the merit of the specific post.

**So:** Mochi may appear beside real work, reacting to something specific, as in Type 2. Mochi
may never carry a post alone with a joke and a logo. If an image contains only the mascot, a
slogan and branding, it is the wrong type.

## Choosing

| Job in the brief | Reach for |
|---|---|
| `laugh`, `curious` | Type 2 — story appeal |
| `proof` | Type 3 — the real artefact, or Type 1 |
| `reassure`, myth-buster, FAQ | Type 1 — the receipt |
| `teach`, carousels | Type 3 — the real screen, one step per slide |
| `invite`, story ads | Type 1 with the loop left open |

One type per image. A receipt that has been illustrated is neither.

## The two rules that are now scoped, not absolute

1. **"No text in the image"** holds for Types 2 and 3. It does not hold for Type 1, where the
   text is the whole image.
2. **The brand palette** holds for Types 2 and 3. Type 1 is deliberately unbranded monochrome.

Everything else in `tt-voice-image-spec.md` still binds: flat shapes, bold outlines, no
gradients, no photorealism where illustration is called for, and the platform aspect ratio.
